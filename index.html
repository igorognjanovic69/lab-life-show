About me

Dr. Nevena Jeremić

Associate Professor of Pharmaceutical Chemistry · redox biology & cardiometabolic research.

I am Dr. Nevena Jeremić, Associate Professor of Pharmaceutical Chemistry at the Faculty of Medical Sciences, University of Kragujevac (Serbia), and Adjunct Assistant Professor at both the University of Louisville (Kentucky, USA) and Sechenov University (Moscow, Russia).

My scientific and professional work focuses on the interface of pharmaceutical chemistry, redox biology, and cardiometabolic research, with a particular interest in molecular mechanisms of cardiovascular protection and translational therapeutic strategies. I have over 18 years of preclinical research experience in experimental pharmacology, drug mechanism studies, and biochemical characterization of novel compounds.

My international research background stems from my work at the University of Louisville, USA, where I gained extensive experience in cardiovascular physiology, redox signaling, and metabolic disease models. I am always open to new national and international collaborations and enjoy working on diverse, innovative, and interdisciplinary projects.

My research focuses on bridging fundamental science and translational medicine, with a particular emphasis on understanding drug–target interactions, redox signaling, and molecular pathways involved in cardiovascular and metabolic diseases. By integrating chemical, biochemical, and physiological approaches, I aim to uncover mechanisms that can guide the development of innovative and mechanism-driven therapeutic strategies with clear clinical relevance and potential patient impact.

      Research focus

      My work sits at the interface of pharmaceutical chemistry, redox biology and cardiometabolic research — with an emphasis on molecular mechanisms of cardiovascular protection and translational therapeutic strategies.

      Cardiovascular protection

      Molecular mechanisms of cardioprotection and ischemia–reperfusion in the isolated heart.

      Redox biology

      Redox signaling and oxidative stress as drivers of disease and therapeutic targets.

      Pharmaceutical chemistry

      Drug–target interactions and biochemical characterization of novel compounds.

      Cardiometabolic disease

      Metabolic syndrome, diabetes and adipose-tissue browning.

      Natural products

      Plant-derived compounds such as diallyl trisulfide (garlic) and Galium verum.

      Preclinical models

      18+ years with rodent models in experimental pharmacology and physiology.
